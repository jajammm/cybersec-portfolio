I'm learning cybersecurity and documenting the journey here. I recently completed the offensive phase of the Dibimbing bootcamp as a penetration tester. Its final project was a full black-box assessment of Dibimbing-Shop, an intentionally vulnerable e-commerce app, in an authorized Dibimbing lab. That assessment produced 10 findings: 4 Critical, 4 High and 2 Moderate — the report on this site walks through all of them. I'm now continuing into the blue team track as a security analyst.

## What I've done — offensive

- **Web application testing**: injection, broken access control (IDOR), session management, authentication flows and business logic.
- **Reconnaissance**: DNS and technology fingerprinting, port scanning, content discovery and manual crawling to map the attack surface before touching anything.
- **Reporting**: CVSS 3.1 scoring, reproducible proof of concept, business impact written for non-technical readers, and remediation developers can actually apply.

I follow the [OWASP Web Security Testing Guide](https://owasp.org/www-project-web-security-testing-guide/), work inside agreed rules of engagement, and keep evidence for every claim.

## What I'm learning now — defensive

Moving into the blue team / security analyst track: log and alert analysis, detection, and incident response — looking at the same weaknesses from my report, but from the defender's side: how they would be spotted and stopped.

## Tools

From the offensive phase: Burp Suite, sqlmap, Nmap, WhatWeb, Gobuster, dirb, Wireshark, curl, nslookup, whois.
